# v0.5.0 Benchmark Baseline Report

## Status

Measured on 2026-09-05. This is the first fixed benchmark baseline for the
implemented runtime. It is suitable for workload-scale and hotspot selection,
but the cross-round drift documented below means future promotion decisions
must use interleaved A/B runs rather than comparing a candidate to these
absolute numbers alone.

The complete machine-readable results are in
[v0.5.0-timings.csv](v0.5.0-timings.csv) and
[v0.5.0-allocations.csv](v0.5.0-allocations.csv). Generated Criterion samples,
JSON, and HTML remain under the ignored `target/criterion/` directory.

Each Criterion target was executed for every round with this command shape:

```text
cargo +stable bench --locked --bench <target> -- --save-baseline <round> --warm-up-time 3 --measurement-time 10 --sample-size 100 --confidence-level 0.99 --significance-level 0.01
```

`<target>` covered `graph_build`, `compile`, `runtime_sync`, `runtime_async`,
`dispatch`, and `async_runtime_integration`. `<round>` covered
`v0.5.0-r1` through `v0.5.0-r3`. Allocations used:

```text

```

## Reliability Notes

- Round 3 was broadly 25-53% slower for every async variant. The shared shape
  across Local, SendMode, Ready, PendingOnce, mixed, and wake-storm workloads
  identifies host-level drift rather than a path-specific regression.
- `compile/full/100` round 2 was a clear isolated outlier: 833.479 us versus
  97.557 us and 88.989 us, with its 99% interval extending to 3.176 ms.
- Serial dispatched chains were noisy: the fixed-pool one-worker point spread
  was about 82%, and the eight-worker spread was about 62%. These measurements
  include queueing and OS scheduling and must not be attributed to graph-core
  execution alone.
- The benchmark-local fixed pool deliberately serializes dequeue behind a
  mutex. Its eight-worker chain result is a reference implementation cost, not
  evidence that more workers intrinsically slow the graph.

## Hotspots and Next Measurements

The baseline establishes that scratch reuse is measurable but not dominant:
the median Local bound chain fell from 59.235 us fresh to 48.477 us reusable,
about 18%. It does not identify the remaining cost by itself. Fresh per-run
cancel/job coordination, per-node input/output construction, erased value
ownership, reference counting, future wrappers, and scheduling bookkeeping all
remain candidates.

The follow-up diagnostic suite used the same I/O style, output consumption, and
operation boundary for A/B/C/D. Complete results are in
[v0.5.0-diagnostics.csv](v0.5.0-diagnostics.csv) and
[v0.5.0-diagnostic-allocations.csv](v0.5.0-diagnostic-allocations.csv).

| Diagnostic | Variant | Median latency | Calls | Requested bytes |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A: 100 active empty nodes | fresh | 12.735 us | 116 | 33,484 |
| A: 100 active empty nodes | reusable | 11.909 us | 103 | 14,568 |
| B: 100-node scalar chain | fresh | 55.394 us | 1,108 | 75,588 |
| B: 100-node scalar chain | reusable | 47.716 us | 901 | 42,752 |
| C: 10x10 fan-out/fan-in with `Many` | fresh | 67.072 us | 1,346 | 115,500 |
| C: 10x10 fan-out/fan-in with `Many` | reusable | 59.209 us | 1,146 | 83,336 |
| D: 100 independent Ready Send nodes | inline reusable | 29.443 us | 609 | 52,680 |
| D: same graph | fixed pool, 1 worker | 56.035 us | 614 | 70,108 |
| D: same graph | fixed pool, 8 workers | 63.576 us | 615 | 71,381 |

The reusable B-A delta is 35.807 us, 798 allocation calls, and 28,184 requested
bytes per frame. The reusable C-B delta is 11.493 us, 245 calls, and 40,584
bytes. C is intentionally a topology stressor, so this delta includes its
additional edges and arithmetic as well as `Many` collection and Shared value
cloning.

D shows that the fixed one-worker adapter adds 26.592 us but only five
allocation calls relative to inline execution. The eight-worker adapter adds
34.133 us and six calls. Submission, synchronization, queueing, wakeups, and OS
scheduling therefore matter more than allocation-call count in this adapter.

WPR CPU capture was attempted for the reusable B workload with the profiling
Cargo profile, but Windows rejected the required system profiling policy with
error `0xc5585011`. No ETL was produced, so no CPU attribution is claimed.
Allocation evidence and source inspection do establish that every synchronous
node currently creates `Box::pin(std::future::ready(...))`. A direct internal
synchronous path is therefore the first isolated implementation experiment.

Accepted and rejected implementation experiments are recorded in
[experiments.md](experiments.md). No candidate is promoted unless three
interleaved A/B rounds satisfy the acceptance gates in
[bench-plan.md](../bench-plan.md) and protected workloads stay within their
regression limits.
